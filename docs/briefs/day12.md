## Why it matters

- **E1:** with no generation metric, a prompt or model change ships blind. The B9 model switch is
  that exact case: moving off `gemini-2.5-flash-lite` might quietly make answers worse or less
  grounded, and today nothing would show it.
- **E2:** take the answer "An LSTM keeps a separate cell state guarded by gates". It paraphrases
  the context faithfully but scores low on word overlap. Now take an answer that repeats a
  retrieved paragraph without answering the question. It scores ≈1.0 on faithfulness and still
  well on "relevancy", because it repeats the question's nouns. The metric rewards the wrong
  answer.
- **Pipeline fidelity:** if the eval builds its own prompt, it measures a prompt users never see.
  Day 11's lesson was the same: measure the system you ship.
- **B9:** production's free tier is 20 requests a day, and a chat turn makes up to 2 calls. Past
  that cap, chat, topic tags, concept maps and quizzes all fail until midnight Pacific. A switch
  measured with no judge risks a quality drop. Measuring with the judge first makes the switch
  safe to ship.

## The concept

### Evaluate generation separately from retrieval
Day 11 asked whether the right pages reached the model. Today asks what the model did with them.
The two failures need different fixes. A wrong answer built from the right context is a prompt or
model problem. A faithful answer built from the wrong context is a retrieval problem. Each judge
therefore looks at a fixed (question, context, answer) triple, and the context is recorded with
the answer, so a bad score can be traced to one stage.

### LLM-as-judge
A second model call grades the first. It works because checking whether sentence X follows from
passage Y is easier than writing a good answer. Known biases:
- **Self-preference:** a judge rates text from its own model family higher.
- **Verbosity:** longer answers look better.
- **Position:** in pairwise comparisons, the first or second option is favoured.
- **Leniency:** "mostly supported" gets rounded up to "supported".

Production practice:
- Use narrow questions with a closed set of answers (labels, not 1–10 scores).
- Run at temperature 0 with structured output and a pinned model version.
- Ask for evidence before the verdict, for example the supporting quote.
- **Calibrate against humans** before trusting any number.

### Claim decomposition (how RAGAS faithfulness works)
"Is this answer faithful? 1–5" is vague and drifts. The robust approach takes three steps:
1. Split the answer into atomic claims.
2. Label each claim `supported` / `contradicted` / `not_in_context` against the context, with
   the quote that supports it.
3. Score faithfulness = supported / total claims.

The three-way verdict matters here because of grounding levels. Under `loose` and `none` the
prompt *allows* general knowledge, so `not_in_context` is permitted there but is a violation under
`strict`, while `contradicted` is a bug under every level. Reporting by grounding band keeps the
permitted fallback from showing up as hallucination.

### Answer relevance and abstention
Relevance asks whether the answer addresses the question that was asked. A refusal ("I am not
sure because the provided context does not contain…") is *correct* for an out-of-corpus question
under strict grounding, and wrong for an answerable one. So relevance gets its own label
(`refusal`), and refusals are reported as an abstention rate, split into negatives (good) and
answerable questions (bad). They are never folded into the mean relevance. RAGAS's own
answer-relevancy metric runs differently: it generates questions back from the answer and embeds
them. That costs more calls and measures something less direct.

### Citation correctness — deterministic first, judge second
A citation has two failure modes:
- **Fabricated:** the cited file or page isn't in the context at all. A regex against the context
  labels checks this for free and exactly.
- **Misattributed:** the cited chunk exists but doesn't support the sentence. Only a judge can tell.

Doing the cheap exact check in code and keeping the judge for what needs language understanding is
a general rule: never spend an LLM call on what a regex can decide.

### Calibration and agreement statistics
Raw agreement is inflated when one label dominates. If 90% of answers are faithful, a judge that
always says "faithful" agrees 90% of the time. **Cohen's κ** corrects for chance:
κ = (pₒ − pₑ)/(1 − pₑ). Rough reading: < 0.4 poor, 0.4–0.6 moderate, 0.6–0.8 substantial,
> 0.8 near-perfect. For ordinal labels (relevance 0–2), weighted κ also gives partial credit for
near misses.

With 20 items, κ's confidence interval is wide. Today's numbers say "the judge is usable" or "it
isn't". They can't separate a κ of 0.7 from 0.8. The human labels must be made **blind** to the
judge's verdicts, or anchoring inflates agreement.

### Comparing two models honestly
For B9, both models answer the **same** questions over the **same** retrieved context. The
retrieval run is deterministic with the cache, so the only variable is the model. The comparison
is **paired**: per-question differences with a bootstrap interval. That is far tighter than two
independent means. A "no drop" claim needs the interval of the difference to sit above a tolerance
we choose in advance (for example −0.05 faithfulness), not just "the means look similar".

### Pitfalls
- A judge that shares the generator's model is grading its own homework: self-preference.
- Judging with the context the eval *thinks* was sent, not what was actually sent.
- Letting `loose` / `none` answers drag down "faithfulness" when the prompt allowed them.
- Re-running a paid or quota-bound judge for every report. Cache verdicts keyed by (judge model,
  prompt version, input hash).
- Free-text judge output parsed by hand. Use schema-constrained JSON (`response_schema`) and
  validate it with Pydantic.

## Decisions

### 1. RAGAS or a Gemini judge (E1) — timeline "Decision"
- **A — Drop RAGAS, build a Gemini judge:** ~250 lines in `eval/generation/` that use the
  `google-genai` client and Pydantic schemas we already have. This removes `ragas`, `datasets` and
  six transitive packages (langchain ×5, openai) along with the dummy-key hack. Trade-off: we own
  the prompts and their calibration, which RAGAS would otherwise maintain.
- **B — Wrap `LLMClient` for RAGAS:** we get RAGAS's metric implementations, but through a
  LangChain wrapper around Gemini. RAGAS's internal prompts also assume a strong OpenAI model. Its
  answer relevancy needs embeddings plus extra calls per answer (≈4–6 calls per question instead
  of 2), which eats into the 500 RPD budget. We would also keep a heavy dependency tree that
  nothing else uses.
- **Recommendation: A.** It is cheaper per question, removes dependencies, and every verdict can be
  inspected. The design copies RAGAS's best idea, claim decomposition.

### 2. How the runner reaches the real generation step
- **A — Extract the shared step:** move ChatPipeline's "context → grounding → prompt" into a
  function, `prepare_answer(...) -> PreparedAnswer` in `app/services/answer_preparation.py`, the
  same pattern as Day 11's `assemble_context`. The chat and the eval both call it, and the answer
  comes from the same `LLMClient.stream_answer`. Trade-off: one more small refactor of
  `chat_pipeline.py`, guarded by the SSE smoke test.
- **B — Compose it in the eval:** the runner calls `assemble_context`, `grounding_level` and
  `build_prompt` itself. ChatPipeline isn't touched, but the two copies can drift, which is the
  failure Day 9 fixed for `/chat`.
- **Recommendation: A.**

### 3. Judge design
- **A — Claim-level, two calls per answer:**
  - *Call 1 (faithfulness + citation support):* the context, labelled the way the model saw it,
    plus the answer. It returns claims, each with a verdict (`supported` / `contradicted` /
    `not_in_context`), a supporting quote and the source the answer cited for it.
  - *Call 2 (relevance):* the question and answer only. It returns a label `full` / `partial` /
    `off_topic` / `refusal` with a one-line reason.
  - *Citation validity* is checked by code, with no call.
  - Trade-off: more structure to validate, but every number traces back to readable claims.
- **B — Holistic 1–5 per dimension, one call:** cheapest (1 call per answer). But 1–5 scores drift
  between runs and models, are hard to calibrate against humans, and don't say *which* sentence was
  unsupported.
- **Recommendation: A.**

### 4. Judge model
- **A — `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`, pinned, temperature 0:** 500 RPD on the eval project, enough for a
  full run plus the B9 comparison in one day. Risk: it judges its own answers in the 3.5 run
  (self-preference). Calibration includes answers from both models, so a bias would show up as
  lower agreement on one of them.
- **B — `gemini-2.5-flash`:** a stronger, separate model, but 20 RPD on the eval project, which is
  10 answers' worth of judging a day. A full run would take a week.
- **Recommendation: A.** The judge model and prompt version are written into every result, so a
  later switch (to a paid stronger judge on Day 26) is visible.

### 5. Settings shape for the model (B9)
- **A — One field, `GEMINI_GENERATION_MODEL`:** used by the answer, topic tag and concept-map
  extractor. One env var on Render. Trade-off: the cosmetic topic tag can't move to a cheaper model
  separately.
- **B — One field per role** (`GEMINI_CHAT_MODEL`, `GEMINI_TOPIC_MODEL`, `GEMINI_CONCEPT_MODEL`):
  flexible, but three variables to keep in sync for no current need.
- **Recommendation: A.** The constructors' hardcoded defaults are removed, so no class silently
  falls back to a model name.

### 6. B9: measuring the before/after under 2.5-flash-lite's 20 RPD, then what production runs
Measuring:

  the question, the context as the model saw it, and the answer, with no judge output. You fill
  `generation_calibration_v1.labels.jsonl`. For each answer you give:
  - per answer, "any unsupported or contradicted claim?" (y/n),
  - relevance (`full` / `partial` / `off_topic` / `refusal`),
  - "every citation points at a chunk that supports its sentence?" (y/n / no citations).
- `calibrate score` gives agreement and κ per dimension, split by generator (the self-preference
  check), and lists every disagreement for us to read.
- If κ is below 0.4 on a dimension, I revise that judge prompt **once** (version bump) and re-score
  from cached answers. Only the judge calls repeat.

## Risks & open questions

- **The 2.5-flash-lite day cap is shared** with anything else run on the eval project's key today.
  If Day 11's tooling ran on it today, the paired subset may get fewer than 20. The cache makes the
  run resumable tomorrow.
- **Self-preference:** the judge is the same model as the "after" generator. Calibration split by
  generator is the check. If agreement differs a lot between the two generators, the B9 comparison
  is reported as biased and we fall back to Decision 6B (billing) or to a second judge.
- **Mode:** the runner uses `quick`, the UI's default (`ChatInput.js:6`). `detailed` answers are
  longer and have more claims, so their faithfulness may differ. One `--mode` flag allows a later
  run.
- **Empty PPTX chunks (B10)** are still in the corpus. If one reaches a context, the judge sees an
  empty labelled block. That is harmless, but it is noted in the results.
- **`gemini-3.5-flash-lite` lifecycle:** if it is a preview model, it can be retired at short
  notice. Can you confirm its status on AI Studio's models page? It changes how safe the B9 switch
  is.
- **Delivery of B9:** the switch reaches users at the Day 17 release (v0.3.0), and production stays
  over quota until then. Do you want a hotfix to `master` instead (the CLAUDE.md hotfix flow) once
  the measurement passes?
- **Calibration data in git:** the labels file holds ids and labels only. The labelling sheet
  (questions, context excerpts from your course files, answers) stays gitignored, like the corpus.
  OK?
- **Lock regeneration** may move nothing but the removed packages; the diff is checked for anything
  else before commit.
